# SOL/USDC research protocol

The question is whether a strategy has repeatable positive net returns under feasible execution. The synthetic replay verifies the decision and ledger path. Reserve historical and paper results for genuine data and use the same conservative assumptions across candidates.

## Freeze the evaluation before tuning

1. Pick a UTC date range, resolution, observation delay, exposure limits, and fee and latency model. Compare uninvested USDC, buy-and-hold SOL with the same capital and SOL exposure ceiling at entry, and low-turnover long/cash momentum on that period. Mark buy-and-hold daily, account for its initial and terminal trade costs, and disclose where an active cap would force an exit. The paper rule starts with a three-sample lookback, an entry of +100 bps and an exit of −50 bps. Research may select a lower-frequency bar rule within the agreed trial budget. Published time-series momentum motivates testing. Its applicability to Solana remains open. [Original time-series momentum study data](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data)
2. Record the hypothesis, parameter list and maximum trials, source and split versions, exact eligibility and metrics, fee assumptions, code revision and seed before looking at results. Divide the earlier data into expanding chronological development folds. Remove training rows whose forward label horizon reaches the next test period, including the final boundary. Use explicit timestamps if bars are irregular. Fit preprocessing within each training fold. Freeze the latest contiguous window as the final untouched holdout. Select one candidate on development evidence, run it once on the holdout, and record that the holdout is spent. Preserve losing runs and log any new trial instead of silently reusing the test tranche. [TimeSeriesSplit](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html) · [The Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
3. Score decisions using inputs actually available at their timestamp. Fill only with a quote or conservative executable proxy observed after the modeled decision and delay. Include slippage, impact, stale quotes, rejected and missed fills, and costs for every entry and exit. Charge separately only for costs outside quoted net amounts. Report assumptions when historical coverage has no size-matched quotes. Add service spend separately and report whether net trading returns also cover it. Use the same start equity and terminal liquidation convention across comparators.
4. Report net P&L in USDC and percent of starting equity, peak-to-trough max drawdown, turnover, fill count and round trips, fill/reject/miss rates per actionable intent, and sensitivity to cost and delay. Record remaining exposure, missing data, and any regime or day concentration. For a fair baseline comparison, record the exact shared timestamps, exposure policy, and cost settings.

### Draft eligibility gates for teammate approval

Freeze these gates or agreed replacements before tuning or viewing outcomes. Reserve at least 90 calendar days for the final holdout and at least 30 calendar days of forward paper. Require at least 30 completed round trips in each phase and max drawdown at most 5% of its prior equity peak. Require positive net P&L and improvement over the applicable frozen cash and buy-and-hold comparators in both phases. An additional model or selector must also beat the approved fixed momentum baseline or each eligible fixed strategy as applicable. Stress with 2× separately estimated costs and incremental adverse execution haircut, and one additional research bar or 10 seconds of paper delay, combined. Require positive net P&L and drawdown within the same limit under stress. Freeze the exact sampling frequency, thresholds, tie rules and delay implementation in the experiment plan. A short phase or thin sample is insufficient evidence. Keep running the approved paper strategy or stay in cash if any gate is missed. Passing gates support a separately reviewed execution-quality trial.

## CPU model and strategy selection

- Start with regularized logistic regression and a short, disclosed list of features such as lagged returns, realized volatility and lagged volume or depth when available. Set a fixed horizon before trials. At decision time `t`, use only data observable by `t`. Begin the label at the first execution proxy after the decision delay. Label 1 if the horizon exit's estimated net proceeds exceed the full entry debit, including round-trip costs, else 0. Mark missing or unavailable labels and disclose exclusions. Purge using the label end time. Fit scaling, missing-value handling, and model weights on each fold’s training data only. Set a trading threshold on development data, then compare after-cost results and drawdown against the same baselines.
- Once two standalone strategies have credible paper records, try a simple trend-and-volatility selector offline. It chooses one approved strategy or cash using observable inputs. If justified, fit a small CPU model scoring each choice with only decision-time features and future simulated net returns as its training labels. Purge overlaps, log the chosen version and its inputs, and count liquidation, entry and any additional switching costs. Compare over identical chronological holdouts with each fixed strategy and cash. Keep a fixed strategy or cash unless switching reliably improves the declared gates.
- Use fixed or reviewed retraining jobs with versioned features, configuration, code, data windows and model artifacts. Once results are repeatable, an optional local batch job may train a candidate once enough new data arrives. Compare against the approved version on the same newly reserved data, then in forward paper. Require teammate approval to switch, record the approved version, and retain the previous version for rollback. LLMs stay outside training and scoring.
- Consider mean reversion under a separate hypothesis after the first pipeline is reliable. Defer arbitrage until speed, impact, and multi-leg costs and failure modes are measured.

## Three PCs, one protocol

Give each distinct candidate or trial group its own issue and assignee. Agree on a total trial budget and allocate non-overlapping trials. Each PC uses its own bounded local slice cache and runs its job independently with the same dataset version, development folds, baseline and cost model. Record experiment ID, dataset and split versions, code revision, configuration, seed if applicable, metrics and failed trials in one compact vault note per experiment. Store larger artifacts in the approved private bucket if needed. Compare development evidence together, then evaluate the selected candidate once on the final holdout. The bot keeps running the approved version throughout.

## Historical data without full local history

1. Use a hosted source such as Dune first. Query the two exact mints in either swap direction and a bounded UTC period remotely. Aggregate to the required bar or feature columns before export. Include availability timestamps and gap/coverage checks. Retain only small derived Parquet snapshots locally. Start with a 256 MiB per-PC cache and a seven-day retention for replaceable slices. Evict oldest unused slices first and record any budget changes. Keep only tiny synthetic fixtures in Git.
2. The dataset maintainer publishes a manifest in Git with dataset ID/version, source/table, saved query or SQL and parameters, mint pair and UTC range, schema and units, row count/gaps and coverage, data freshness and observation delay, SHA-256 checksums, extraction date, and allowed storage/sharing uses. Estimate query scan or compute credits, export/download credits, and storage and request costs from the provider plan and a small measured query before scaling. Check account quotas, retention rules and redistribution terms before a paid run. Keep charges in the service budget and bring the estimate to the owner. [Dune billing](https://docs.dune.com/api-reference/overview/billing)
3. When shared snapshots or repeated exports justify it, publish immutable versioned Parquet into one private S3-compatible bucket such as R2, partitioned by UTC date. Query only needed dates and columns with DuckDB `httpfs`. Give each teammate individual read access and only the dataset maintainer write access. Keep SQL, schemas and manifests in Git and private locations and credentials in local config or a secret store. Keep only small results locally. Runtime SQLite stays on one local disk with one writer and stays outside Git and vault sync. [DuckDB S3 support](https://duckdb.org/docs/stable/core_extensions/httpfs/s3api) · [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)

## Public-wallet following as a paper experiment

Freeze the cohort from only information available before the evaluation period. For covered Jupiter activity use `jupiter_solana.aggregator_swaps` and deduplicate by `(tx_id, log_index)`, the transaction ID and swap index. Venue legs may describe one economic swap and must never each trigger a copy. Historical block timestamps alone cannot establish live observability. Before any forward test, measure a separate Solana event feed for detection, confirmation, missing events and availability. Record when the follower would first observe each eligible event, then price its own size-matched delayed quote. Model drift, failed and missed entries and exits, fee and liquidity differences, and partial visibility into the leader’s holdings and other activity. Include cohort selection bias and missing/non-Jupiter activity. Cap allocation per source wallet in addition to the strategy’s mint and total exposure caps. Apply the same net-return, delay and cost stress gates. Reject following if the follower fails.
